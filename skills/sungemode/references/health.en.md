# Health and compounding


## Interview ideas and limits

- **Carbon-based server and sustained action:** Device maintenance is a metaphor, not a medical mechanism or causal proof.
- **Quantified life:** Food photos, sleep/activity logs, and wearables support observation. Calorie expenditure is estimated; device labels are not clinical diagnoses.
- **Proactive management:** Attend to habits and risks; testing and treatment require individual context and reliable medical evidence.
- **Lean physique and body image:** Appearance preferences, performance, and health are separate; no body type defines health or personal worth.
- **Youth, time, longevity:** A longer potential horizon is an opportunity argument, not a lifespan or technological-extension guarantee.

## Application: project-designed method

Establish the desired improvement, feasible routine, relevant records, and restrictions. With sparse data, begin brief logging or general habits without demanding complete health data. Verify concrete advice against reliable public-health/professional sources; do not derive doses, caloric targets, or treatment from subtitles.

Habit trials assess feasibility, logs, and subjective experience. Do not infer health effects from short-term weight changes, wearable scores, or income. Pause new exercise for significant discomfort and seek appropriate help for symptoms; a symptom request requires separate handling from routine habit planning.

Deliver observed facts versus estimates, one sustainable habit/logging action, workload, and adjustment conditions. Shared plans must protect sleep and recovery rather than sacrifice them for business experiments.

## Body maintenance: locate the first failing part of the plan

Use this for routines and observation, not symptom assessment or treatment.

1. Identify the desired experience: less fatigue, a feasible routine, sustainable activity, or appearance. Keep goals distinct.
2. Inspect a typical day, obligations, opportunities for rest, existing activity, and preparation costs. Check overcommitment before attributing failure to discipline.
3. Choose one adjustable part: protect existing walking time, simplify logging, or change evening work arrangements. Verify concrete exercise/nutrition advice separately.
4. Define a minimum feasible version and fallback. When work expands, reduce new tasks rather than compensate by sacrificing rest.
5. Observe workload, execution, and subjective experience. Review feasibility; physiological outcomes need suitable timescales/evidence, not short-term causal claims.

Deliver the original conflict, proposed schedule change, time cost, pause/help conditions, and review date. Missing schedule/symptom data require conditional branches, not assumed sleep deprivation or inactivity.

For “good income but exhausted,” check work duration, caregiving, displaced recovery, and symptoms requiring professional attention. Reducing side-project goals may be more useful than adding exercise tasks.
