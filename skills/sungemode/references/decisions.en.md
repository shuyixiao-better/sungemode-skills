# Decisions and exploration


## Interview ideas and limits

- **Scout first, validate cheaply:** Gather information that can change a decision. Low-cost experiments are our interpretation of the map metaphor.
- **Global perspective and following opportunities:** Include language, qualifications, immigration, tax, caregiving, and moving costs. Stability and local accumulated knowledge also have value.
- **Interest, expertise, sustained effort:** Explore before specializing; interest is not proof of capability.
- **Feedback, information value, investigation limits:** Judge research by improved choices, with a stopping rule instead of endless collection.
- **Irreversible commitments, small trials, starting constraints:** Preserve room to change. Fewer prior commitments can ease switching, but poverty restricts affordable experimentation.

## Application: project-designed method

Compare the status quo and a few alternatives by goal, cash/time, reversibility, responsibilities, and critical unknown. Eliminate hard-constraint violations before investigating what could change the ranking. Separate short trials from quitting or relocating.

Deliver an option table, conditional preference, next information-gathering step/trial, affordable downside, and review/research-stop date. Validate employment outcomes independently; payment metrics do not apply to health or relationships.

## Scouting: from unknowns to the next commitment

Adapt this process to one decision:

1. Define the commitment, deadline, and irreversible consequences. Exploring an interest and quitting are different commitments.
2. Map options including status quo, entry requirements, resources, and obligations. Separate claimed and demonstrated skills.
3. Find unknowns that change ranking. State how answer A/B changes the choice; deprioritize information that cannot affect it.
4. Send one scout: an affordable investigation or experience with an information goal, time/cash cap, and expected evidence. Do not invent completed interviews, applications, or experiences.
5. Reassess after evidence: investigate, trial, commit, or retain status quo. At the review date, missing critical evidence can justify delay or a reversible route, not invented scores.

For career changes, a scout may inspect real job requirements, produce a small artifact, or ask a practitioner when authorized. Choose one based on resources, not automatic course purchases or outreach.

Deliver: “Before committing X, use Y to learn Z; observation A supports continuing, B calls for adjustment, missing evidence calls for delay.” Propose amounts within user constraints without accepting losses on their behalf.

## Following opportunities: switching conditions, not constant relocation

Compare actual opportunities, entry conditions, accumulated value, and responsibilities in current/alternative environments. Specify changes warranting reassessment, such as sustained demand decline, changed qualifications, or care arrangements. Consider remote exploration, short trials, gradual changes, or stability. Flexibility preserves options without requiring relocation. Verify job, immigration, and tax facts before relying on them; environmental mismatch is not automatically personal failure.
