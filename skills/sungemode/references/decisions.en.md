# Decisions and exploration


## Interview ideas and limits

- **Scout first, validate cheaply:** Gather information that can change a decision. Low-cost experiments are our interpretation of the map metaphor.
- **Global perspective and following opportunities:** Include language, qualifications, immigration, tax, caregiving, and moving costs. Stability and local accumulated knowledge also have value.
- **Interest, expertise, sustained effort:** Explore before specializing; interest is not proof of capability.
- **Feedback, information value, investigation limits:** Judge research by improved choices, with a stopping rule instead of endless collection.
- **Irreversible commitments, small trials, starting constraints:** Preserve room to change. Fewer prior commitments can ease switching, but poverty restricts affordable experimentation.

## Application: project-designed method

Compare the status quo and a few alternatives by goal, cash/time, reversibility, responsibilities, and critical unknown. Eliminate hard-constraint violations before investigating what could change the ranking. Separate short trials from quitting or relocating.

Deliver an option table, conditional preference, next information-gathering step/trial, affordable downside, and review/research-stop date. Validate employment outcomes independently; payment metrics do not apply to health or relationships.
