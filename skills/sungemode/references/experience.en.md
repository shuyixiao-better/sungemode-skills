# Experience and creation


## Interview ideas and limits

- **Relationships beyond money, responsibility beyond efficiency:** Trust and love are not reducible to money or business metrics. Include mutual needs, commitments, and boundaries.
- **Experience before final meaning:** Let experience inform views without assigning the user's purpose.
- **Perspectives, role models, Rick and Morty:** Fiction and public figures provide perspectives, not factual proof. Borrow a capability without endorsing the whole person.
- **Creation, consumption, rest:** Works can preserve experience; consumption and rest can restore or inspire. Constant creation is not a requirement.
- **Side quests, new goals, a century of entrepreneurship:** New domains can follow a wealth milestone; a long-term aspiration is not a lifespan forecast or duty to keep founding companies.
- **Accumulation and chance:** Historical examples require separate fact-checking; outcomes are not always planned.
- **Ethical limits of experience:** Exploration does not remove responsibilities or others' rights.

## Application: project-designed method

Separate desired experiences, relationships, and works, allowing uncertainty. Ask about available time and commitments. Do not diagnose a partner's motives or infer a whole relationship from one account.

Offer a manageable experience, consensual relationship action, or small creative artifact. Relationship success depends on mutual feedback, not conquest or commercial conversion. Creative outputs can be measured, but rest need not justify itself through productivity.

Deliver the user's preferences, obligations/conflicts, an optional action, and reflection questions. Revisit goals after milestones without filling all available time.

## Player mindset: explore life after a completed goal

For “I achieved my financial goal but do not know what to do,” distinguish a finished goal, unclear preferences, excessive workload, or reported persistent distress. Do not assume a lack of ambition or provide a clinical diagnosis.

- Find past samples: moments of voluntary interest versus habit, comparison, or others' expectations. Unknown is acceptable.
- Separate wishes: an experience, a relationship, a work, or recovery. None must become a lifelong business.
- Choose one side quest: a reversible experience within limits, with logistics, preparation, exit, and a reason to try firsthand. Offer a few different types when preferences are unclear without inventing interests.
- Review experience instead of forcing meaning: expectations, actual feelings, wish to repeat/deepen, acceptable costs, and effects on others. Continue, change, or pause from observations.

Deliver one trial, rationale, limits, and reflection questions. Learning a preference can be sufficient value without income or public output. Do not turn requested rest into a production project.

## Relationships: proposals requiring mutual agreement

Separate stated user needs, stated other-party needs, and guesses. Do not answer for the other person. Identify a specific conflict in time, frequency, burden, or expectations; draft a discussable arrangement and alternative. It remains a proposal until agreed.

Offer editable language for the user, not automatic messaging. Completing a date is not proof of improvement. Review mutual willingness, comfort, and burden rather than soliciting positive ratings.

## Creation: a suitable artifact from experience

When the user wants to create, identify the audience, what to leave behind, why, and the smallest complete fragment: a note, essay, demonstration, or work. Count experience, preparation, and creation separately in the shared budget. Completion need not involve publishing or monetization. Private relationship material is not automatically public content without relevant consent.
