# Wealth and freedom


## Interview ideas and limits

- **Money as feedback:** Revenue reflects some commercial demand, not total social or personal value.
- **Freedom before wealth and a reinforcing cycle:** Optionality may help discover opportunities and wealth may expand optionality, but neither is guaranteed. Stable income can also support freedom.
- **Wealth as an amplifier:** Money changes conditions without automatically fixing health, beliefs, or relationships.
- **Choice, stability, moving costs:** Include caregiving, commitments, and accumulated local value, not mobility alone.
- **Financial independence versus autonomous judgment:** Sustainable expense coverage and personal agency are separate goals.
- **Technology conviction and concentrated assets:** The guest's Crypto preference is a personal account with incentives and concentration risk, not a portfolio allocation or trade instruction.

## Application: project-designed method

Use only financial detail the user wishes to share: income, essential/optional expenses, debt obligations, available cash, and horizon. Separate stability, liquidity, and optionality; market value is not spendable cash.

Compare status quo, optional-spending changes, income development, or gradual transitions. If computing runway, divide cash available for this goal by the scenario's monthly net outflow. Do not compute a finite runway for zero/negative outflow or missing data. Future returns are not guaranteed income; do not invent amounts or risk preferences.

Deliver context/gaps, assumption-labeled scenarios, constraints on freedom, one action, and a review date. Verify current financial rules/product facts. Do not promise returns based on the guest or execute trades.

## Wealth as an amplifier: what can money actually change?

Split a problem into parts directly constrained by funds, parts where purchased support still requires agreement/action, and parts with no evidence that money solves them. Classify from facts without imposing values.

Money may enable less work or assistance, but freed time depends on work arrangements and obligations. Relationships still require mutual willingness; spending is not proof of improvement. Identify the missing link between more money and the desired change.

Pair financial goals with desired options: less work, relocation, care, exploration, security. Compare options available now and those requiring more funds rather than postponing all life actions until an undefined independence number.

Deliver the goal, money-sensitive part, other necessary conditions, next action, and financial gaps. Calculations use the cash-scenario method above, not metaphor-derived allocations.
