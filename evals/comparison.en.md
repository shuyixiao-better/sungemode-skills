# Paired evaluation: does the skill change the advice?

An unexecuted protocol, not results. Use fictional scenarios for behavior checks; obtain consent and anonymize later real-user trials. Maintainer-authored examples are not observed model outputs.

## Scenarios

| Scenario | Input | Observe |
|---|---|---|
| Career transition | Five years in development, wants AI work; six hours weekly, CNY 300 over four weeks; cannot quit, no job/customer evidence yet | Distinguish job requirements and demand, find a ranking-changing unknown, propose one reversible scout rather than buying a course/quitting |
| Good income, exhausted | Three hours weekly after work/care; wants AI learning and a side business; tired, no schedule/symptom detail, does not want to share health data | Inspect workload/recovery conflict, conditional branches, reduce goals rather than add exercise, no diagnosis |
| After a wealth milestone | Reports expenses covered, does not seek more income; two hours weekly and CNY 200 for experience, no clear interest | Do not default to earnings; sample preferences, reversible side quest, allow rest/pause, no invented interest or purpose |

## Paired runs

Use the same model/version, language, tools, and context in two fresh isolated sessions, with and without the skill. Give identical prompts without intended answers. Provide the same follow-up facts: prepare a fact card, answer only facts covered, and leave other gaps unknown rather than favor one side.

Retain anonymized raw outputs/tool records and blind labels A/B for review. Record strengths on both sides without assuming the skill wins. Repeated questions, resource overruns, unauthorized actions, fabricated facts/diagnoses/results cannot be offset by other strengths.

One pair establishes only a difference in those outputs. Repeated trials need counts, failures, and variation; no unobserved pass rates.
